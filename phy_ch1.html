📜 Historical Background of Electrostatics

The word Electrostatics originates from the Greek word elektron (meaning amber) and statikos (meaning stationary/staying still). Around 600 BCE, the ancient Greek philosopher Thales of Miletus discovered that when amber was rubbed against animal fur, it mysteriously acquired the power to attract light objects like feathers, dust, and dry leaves.

Centuries later, in the late 18th century, pioneering scientists like Benjamin Franklin, Charles-Augustin de Coulomb, and Michael Faraday transformed these curiosities into precise mathematical frameworks, proving that electric forces hold atoms together and govern lightning flashes in the sky! ⚡

📖 Simplified Notes & Core Concepts

1. Electric Charge ($q$): Charge is an intrinsic property of subatomic particles (protons are positive $+e$, electrons are negative $-e$). Like charges repel each other, while opposite charges attract like magnets. 🧲

2. Coulomb's Law: The electrostatic force ($F$) between two stationary point charges ($q_1$ and $q_2$) separated by distance ($r$) in a vacuum is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

$$F = \frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$$

3. Electric Field Lines ($\vec{E}$): An imaginary line drawn in an electric field such that the tangent at any point gives the direction of the electric field intensity. Field lines always start on positive charges and terminate on negative charges. They never cross each other!

4. Gauss's Law: Total electric flux ($\Phi_E$) passing through any closed hypothetical surface (Gaussian surface) is equal to the total charge ($q_{enc}$) enclosed by that surface divided by the permittivity of free space ($\varepsilon_0$).

$$\Phi_E = \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

5. Capacitance ($C$): Capacitance is the ability of a body to store electrical charge per unit potential difference. For a parallel plate capacitor, it depends on plate area ($A$), distance ($d$), and dielectric medium ($\varepsilon$).

$$C = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$
